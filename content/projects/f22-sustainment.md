As lead Program Manager for Northrop Grumman's F-22 repairs line, I ran a
multi-year, multimillion-dollar effort to maintain and repair advanced avionics
for Lockheed Martin and the U.S. Air Force. The hardware was software-defined
radios and power amplifier modules. The job combined technical delivery,
contract negotiation and direct profit-and-loss accountability, and I held a
security clearance throughout.

## Running the line

I directed execution of the radio and power amplifier module repairs, keeping
engineering, production and logistics aligned across internal teams and outside
partners. On top of the repair work itself, the program carried full lifecycle
management for several new development efforts. Those were capabilities that
did not exist yet and had to be built while the existing line kept delivering.

## The customer relationship

I was the primary interface with Lockheed Martin, responsible for technical
delivery and weekly performance reporting. I also worked directly with
customers across several external agencies to manage expectations and report
status and cost, and supported multiple program-level audits from outside
agencies.

## Winning the follow-on

Using past actuals and a hands-on sense of what the work genuinely cost, I
built the estimates, wrote the proposal, and negotiated directly with the
customer to win a $25M five-year follow-on repair contract. It secured
long-term revenue and meaningfully expanded the program's footprint.

## Reporting up

I reported Earned Value Management, schedule performance and program
profitability to Northrop Grumman VP leadership. Accurate forecasting was the
job. On a repair line, an optimistic schedule quietly becomes a cost problem
two quarters later.

## Leading the teams

The program ran on engineering skill sets pulled from across disciplines. I led
those teams to hit repair timelines, reduce operational bottlenecks, and keep
output quality steady over a multi-year effort.
